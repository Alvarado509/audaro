Audaro started with a simple frustration: editing one audio file online meant uploading it to a stranger’s server, waiting in a queue, and creating an account for a 30-second job.

What we believe

Your audio is yours. A ringtone, a voice note, a podcast episode, a song you are remixing — none of it needs to travel to a server to be trimmed or converted. Modern browsers can do all of it, on your device, in seconds.

So Audaro does exactly that. Every tool runs 100% in your browser with the Web Audio API. There is no upload, no account, no install, and no waiting room. You drop a file, you get a result.

What Audaro is — and is not

Audaro is a set of honest, focused tools: cut, remove vocals, convert, remove silence, boost volume, join, and change speed. Each one does one job well, and says plainly what it cannot do. Our vocal remover, for example, uses phase cancellation on stereo tracks — it works well on centered vocals, and it is not studio-grade AI separation. We would rather tell you that upfront than oversell it.

Audaro is not a cloud studio, not a social network, and not a place that keeps your files. If you need heavy AI separation or a full multitrack editor, a desktop app is still the right call — and we will say so on the tool page.

How Audaro makes money

The free plan is supported by ads, and a Premium plan ($5/month) adds comfort: no ads, batch processing, and large files. Premium payments are not open yet — see the pricing page.
